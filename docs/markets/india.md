# Source market: India

> Counselling analysis for Expert Education & Visa Services counsellors and for Edge. Researched October 2026. Anything marked "as of" can change. Check the linked official source before quoting it to a student. Where a claim rests only on press or consultancy reporting, the text says so.

## 1. Student profile & goals

**Scale and direction.** India is the largest source market for most of Expert's destinations. Ministry of External Affairs figures reported in Parliament (December 2025) put Indian higher-education students abroad at about 1.25 million in 2025. That is the first fall after three years of growth, down from about 1.33 million in 2024 ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/outbound-indian-university-enrolments-fall-after-three-year-rise/), as of Dec 2025). India was still the top sender to the US in 2024/25, with 363,019 students ([IIE Open Doors 2025](https://www.iie.org/news/open-doors-2025-press-release/)).

**Motivation varies a lot by segment.** Counsellors should identify the segment early:

| Segment | Typical goal | Typical level / field | Notes |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Punjab / Haryana (and Doaba belt)** | Settlement or PR is often the explicit goal, with study as the route | Diplomas, PG diplomas, college programmes; often after Class 12 or a 3-year degree | Canada has been the main draw because of its PR pathway ([ThePrint](https://theprint.in/ground-reports/punjab-canada-dream-student-immigration-crackdown/3022955/)). A PAU field study of rural Punjab migration (students and workers) reported that about 56% of migrant households borrowed to send family abroad ([Asia Samachar summary](https://asiasamachar.com/2024/01/15/panjabis-borrowed-sold-assets-worth-rs20-crore-to-migrate-to-canada-dubai-study/)). |
| **Gujarat** | Mixed. Settlement, family-business links, diaspora networks | UG and PG business, IT, health | Some Australian universities named Gujarat among regions with high attrition or refusal in 2023 (see section 6). |
| **Andhra Pradesh / Telangana** | Work after a degree. US STEM master's then OPT/H-1B is the classic route | MS in CS, data science, engineering | Students from the two states received 56% of US student visas issued to Indians in 2023, per the US Consulate as reported by [Dinamalar](https://www.dinamalar.com/news/kalvimalar-news-en/-ap-telangana-dominate-us-student-visas-in-2023/52937). Brookings found some students from these states ending up at unaccredited institutions (cited in the same reporting). |
| **Kerala** | Mixed. Work, nursing and health careers, settlement. Shifting from the Gulf to Western destinations | Nursing, health, PG after an Indian UG degree | Kerala Migration Survey 2023 (reported): student emigrants rose from about 129,763 (2018) to about 250,000 (2023). More than 80% of students over 16 went abroad after graduating in Kerala ([The South First](https://thesouthfirst.com/kerala/youth-exodus-kerala-sees-unprecedented-rise-in-student-emigration-sparks-concerns-of-growing-brain-drain/)). |
| **Metro / upper-middle class (Delhi NCR, Mumbai, Bengaluru, Pune, Chennai, Hyderabad)** | Prestige, career and ranking-led. Degree-and-return is more plausible | Top-ranked UG, MBA/MiM, specialist master's | More likely self-funded or able to borrow without collateral. Rankings and employer outcomes drive the choice. |

**Age and gap patterns.** Most applicants are either (a) Class 12 leavers going to UG or diploma, or (b) graduates aged 21–25 going to a master's. Many PG applicants have one to several years of work or a study gap. Expect visa officers to ask about gaps, so collect evidence (employment letters, payslips, EPF records, certificates) up front.

**How goals change the advice.** A settlement-motivated student and a degree-and-return student need different destination advice. Edge should record the student's real goal and must not coach a student to hide it. Genuine-student and intent tests (AU Genuine Student, UK credibility, US 214(b)) assess what the applicant says against their circumstances.

## 2. Family & decision-making

- **Who decides and pays.** The family, usually the father or both parents, often with input from extended family, normally funds and approves the decision. Relatives abroad (common in Punjab, Gujarat and Kerala) are also influential. Sponsors are typically parents, sometimes grandparents, uncles or siblings. Each extra sponsor adds evidence work (relationship proof, income source).
- **Involving parents.** Hold at least one session with parents present before final destination and lender choices. Parents usually need the total cost (tuition, living costs, visa, insurance, TCS, flights) and the loan EMI timeline explained, not just the tuition fee. Get both student and sponsor consent before sharing documents or creating loan applications.
- **Communication norms.** WhatsApp is the default channel. Parents often prefer phone calls for decisions. Expect negotiation on fees and a strong "what are the PR chances" question. Answer it honestly, with sources, and do not promise outcomes.
- **Language for drafts.** English is fine for most students. For parent-facing messages, offer Hindi or the family's regional language: Punjabi (Gurmukhi), Gujarati, Telugu, Malayalam, Tamil, Marathi, Bengali or Kannada. Keep official names (CAS, CoE, I-20, GIC, LRS, TCS) in English inside translated text.

## 3. Academic background

**School boards.** The main boards are CBSE, CISCE (ICSE for Class 10, ISC for Class 12), the state boards, and NIOS. Marks are percentages. CBSE also reports grades and does not publish an aggregate "division", so destinations usually compute the percentage from the best subjects or all subjects, depending on the institution.
- **Results timing (as of 2026).** CBSE Class 12 results were reported released on 13 May 2026 ([Lingaya's Vidyapeeth notice](https://www.lingayasvidyapeeth.edu.in/?p=17845), secondary source). From 2026 CBSE runs two Class 10 board exams. The first is compulsory in February, an optional improvement sitting follows in May, and the better score counts ([IANS](https://ianslive.in/cbse-announces-two-board-exams-for-class-10-from-2026--20250625172127)). Many September intakes therefore get final Class 12 marks only in May–June. Conditional offers are normal.

**Degrees.**
- **3-year vs 4-year.** Traditional BA, BCom and BSc degrees are 3 years. BE, BTech and some professional degrees are 4 years. Under NEP 2020 the UGC curriculum framework adds 4-year honours and honours-with-research options with multiple exit points, but rollout differs by state and university ([Kerala FYUGP review report](https://plutusias.com/?p=234064)). Check which track a transcript shows.
- **How destinations read a 3-year degree.** UK and Australian universities generally accept a 3-year Indian bachelor's for master's entry (institution-specific). US graduate schools and Canadian universities vary. WES treats a 3-year degree as equivalent to a US/Canadian bachelor's only when it is first division from an NAAC "A"-graded institution. Other 3-year degrees evaluate as three years of undergraduate study ([WES help article](https://us-help.wes.org/support/solutions/articles/14000126526-does-wes-recognize-a-3-year-bachelor-s-degree-from-india-)). Individual universities can be stricter, for example [Santa Clara University](https://www.scu.edu/globalengagement/about/policies-procedures-and-guidelines/three-year-undergraduate-degrees-from-outside-of-the-us/).
- **Backlogs (ATKT).** Arrears or re-sits appear on semester marksheets. Many universities cap the number of backlogs they accept, and the caps differ by institution and country. Always ask for all semester marksheets, including failed attempts. Hiding backlogs is a misrepresentation risk. I could not verify a single current cap that applies across destinations.
- **CGPA conversions.** Indian universities use 10-point CGPA scales with institution-specific percentage formulas (some print a multiplier on the transcript). Use the formula the university prints. Do not apply a generic multiplier.

**Credential evaluation.** WES accepts consolidated transcripts or yearly marksheets in a sealed, institution-endorsed envelope. Since August 2021, India-educated applicants can carry the sealed envelopes themselves in most cases. Photocopies of transcripts are not accepted. For most master's degrees, WES also needs the bachelor's records ([WES Indian credential evaluations](https://www.wes.org/resource-library/blog/credential-advice/wes-indian-credential-evaluations/)). Germany requires an **APS certificate** from APS India, based at the German Embassy in New Delhi since November 2022 ([Fintiba](https://www.fintiba.com/germany/visa/aps-certificate-for-student-visa), secondary).

**MOI letters.** Many UK and Australian providers accept a Medium of Instruction letter from the Indian university, or Class 12 English marks, instead of IELTS/PTE for admission. This is provider policy and does not by itself satisfy visa English rules, which differ by country. Confirm each provider's current policy.

## 4. Money

**Remittance rules.**
- **RBI Liberalised Remittance Scheme (LRS).** A resident individual can remit up to **USD 250,000 per financial year (April–March)** for permitted current or capital account transactions, which include study abroad ([RBI Master Direction, LRS](https://www.rbi.org.in/Scripts/BS_ViewMasterDirections.aspx?id=10192)). The remittance is made through an Authorised Dealer bank with Form A2 and the admission and fee documents.
- **TCS on education remittances (FY 2026-27, from 1 April 2026).** Nothing up to **₹10 lakh** a financial year. Above ₹10 lakh: **2%** on education remittances (cut from 5%), and 20% stays for non-education purposes ([Budget Speech 2026-27, para 104](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/Budget_Speech.pdf); [Finance Bill 2026 memorandum, s. 394(1) Income-tax Act 2025, effective 1 April 2026](https://www.indiabudget.gov.in/doc/memo.pdf)).
- **Loan-funded remittances.** Budget 2025 removed TCS on education remittances funded by a loan from a specified financial institution ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/amp/budget/news/tcs-on-foreign-remittances-up-to-rs-10-lakh-none-on-foreign-education-loan-125020100857_1.html)). The 2026 memorandum does not change that.
- **Status check.** These are the Finance Bill 2026 proposals. *As of 9 Oct 2026, the final enacted text of the Finance Act 2026 is not verified* from an official source, so ask the student's AD bank which rate it is applying. TCS can be credited against the remitter's income tax, so tell parents it is not a pure cost.

**Education loans.**
- **Public-sector banks.** SBI Global Ed-Vantage is the flagship overseas loan. SBI reportedly raised its collateral-free limit to ₹50 lakh for a select list of institutions ([Business Standard, Jan 2025](https://www.business-standard.com/finance/personal-finance/taking-a-loan-to-study-abroad-be-prepared-to-pay-margin-money-of-10-25-125012301320_1.html)). Current rates and bands are on [SBI's rate page](https://sbi.bank.in/web/interest-rates/interest-rates/loan-schemes-interest-rates/education-loan-scheme). Other public-sector banks (Bank of Baroda, Canara, PNB, Union Bank) have similar schemes.
- **Private banks and NBFCs.** Private banks (HDFC, ICICI, Axis) and education-focused NBFCs (e.g. HDFC Credila, Avanse, Auxilo, InCred) are common alternatives. In practitioner experience they are often faster and need less collateral, usually at higher rates. That is a counsellor observation, not a sourced figure, so compare sanctioned terms on each lender's own site.
- **UK visa and Indian loan letters.** UKVI accepts a student loan as proof of funds when it comes from a government, a government-sponsored loan company, or an "academic or educational loans scheme" run by a lender regulated for student loans by the official regulator in its own country ([Appendix Finance, FIN 8.3(c)](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-finance)). Indian banks and RBI-registered NBFCs are RBI-regulated, so their education-loan schemes can qualify. UKVI publishes no list of accepted Indian lenders, so whether a given NBFC's letter is accepted is *not verified*; check with the university's CAS compliance team.
  - **What the letter must show (FIN 9.2):** it is dated no more than 6 months before the application; the loan is to the applicant; the amount; the lender type; that funds are available before the student travels, or are paid to the university with living costs available on arrival; and that the only release condition is a successful UK Student visa application ([Appendix Finance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-finance)).
  - **Not acceptable:** an "in-principle" or provisional letter, a letter that needs further collateral or conditions after the visa, or a loan in the parent's name. The 28-day holding rule is for money in an account; a qualifying loan letter is a separate kind of evidence ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money)).
- **Government portal.** [Vidya Lakshmi](https://www.vidyalakshmi.co.in/) lets students submit one common application to several banks. The Padho Pardesh interest subsidy for minority students studying abroad was **discontinued from 2022-23** ([The Wire](https://m.thewire.in/article/government/padho-pardesh-scheme-discontinued)). Do not quote it as available.

**Proof-of-funds practice and red flags.**
- Accepted forms usually include sanctioned (not just "in-principle") loan letters, savings and fixed deposits, PF/PPF, and sponsor income evidence (ITRs, Form 16, CA statements). Holding periods differ by destination. The UK requires 28 consecutive days ending within 31 days of application ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money)), and Canada asks for 6 months of statements showing the source ([IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/financial-support.html)).
- **Red flags officers look for:** large unexplained deposits just before applying; CA "net-worth certificates" with no underlying documents; agricultural income that is not backed by land records or sale receipts; sponsors whose declared income cannot plausibly support the cost; and loan letters from small cooperative banks that cannot be verified. Canada's suspect-financial-document cases (see section 7) show these are prosecuted.

**Currency.** Fees are paid in the destination currency. INR depreciation between offer and payment changes the real cost, so budget a buffer and show parents a sensitivity range.

| **Police Clearance Certificate (PCC)** | Apply on [Passport Seva](https://www.passportindia.gov.in/), then visit a PSK or POPSK | Since 2022, PCC applications for education are also accepted at Post Office PSKs ([Udayavani](https://www.udayavani.com/english-news/passport-applicants-can-apply-online-for-police-clearance-certificates)). Documents go through e-Sanad, where the issuing body is integrated (verification by the issuing body, then MEA apostille), or through pre-authentication by the state or UT and MEA's outsourced agencies ([MEA e-Sanad note](https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/attach/e_sanad_website.pdf)). | **Biometrics / VAC** | VFS Global VACs across Indian cities for AU, UK, CA, NZ, Ireland, Germany | Book through the destination's official portal, not a third party. US applicants use the US visa scheduling system and the embassy or consulates (New Delhi, Mumbai, Chennai, Hyderabad, Kolkata). |
| **Medicals** | Panel physicians: [Australia](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/help-support/meeting-our-requirements/health/arrange-your-health-examinations), [Canada](https://secure.cic.gc.ca/pp-md/pp-list.aspx), [UK TB test](https://www.gov.uk/tb-test-visa/countries-where-you-need-a-tb-test-to-enter-the-uk) | India is on the UK TB-test list, and tests are valid only at approved clinics ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/tb-test-visa/countries-where-you-need-a-tb-test-to-enter-the-uk)). |
| **Loan disbursement & LRS** | AD bank | Get Form A2, the TCS treatment and a loan-funded remittance declaration done before the fee deadline. |

## 6. Destination-by-destination guidance

**Australia**
- **Evidence level (as of Oct 2026).** Australia moved India from Evidence Level 2 to **Evidence Level 3** on **8 January 2026**, citing "emerging integrity risks" (along with Nepal, Bangladesh and Bhutan). EL3 normally means full financial and English evidence ([AHC Lawyers](https://www.ahclawyers.com/news-articles/26/01/13/student-visa-subclass-500-update-evidence-level-change-for-south-asian-students-explained); [Khaleej Times](https://www.khaleejtimes.com/world/australia-moves-india-into-highest-risk-category-student-visas?amp=1)). Home Affairs does not publish the country list. Consultancies report a further evidence-level revision for applications from 27 March 2026, and I could not verify India's level after it. Always run the official [Document Checklist Tool](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/web-evidentiary-tool) with the actual CRICOS provider.
- **Processing priority.** Ministerial Direction 115 has applied since 14 November 2025. It prioritises offshore applications by how far the provider is through its 2026 allocation, under a national planning level of 295,000 new commencements ([Study Australia](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/Agent-Hub/agent-news-index/ministerial-direction-115)). Provider choice now affects processing speed.
- **Genuine Student requirement.** This replaced the GTE test from 23 March 2024 ([Collegedunia summary](https://collegedunia.com/australia/article/new-australia-student-visa-processing-rules-2026)). Answers must match the course, career and family-tie evidence.
- **Provider-level state restrictions (as reported).** These are individual universities' decisions, not government rules. In April–May 2023, Federation, Western Sydney, Victoria, Edith Cowan, Torrens and Southern Cross universities paused or restricted students from Punjab, Haryana, Gujarat, UP, Uttarakhand and/or J&K ([The Tribune, 24 May 2023](https://www.tribuneindia.com/news/diaspora/australian-universities-ban-indian-students-from-punjab-gujarat-and-haryana-amid-visa-fraud-concerns-510818)). Current lists are sent privately to agents (*not verified as of Oct 2026*), so check the provider's latest agent bulletin.
- **Common refusal reasons.** Weak GS answers (course unrelated to background, "cheap college" choices); unexplained gaps; financial evidence that does not hold up; and course hopping (agents are now barred from commission on onshore transfers, see section 7).

**United Kingdom**
- India is **not** on the UK differential-evidence (low-risk) list, so full financial and academic evidence is required ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money)). Maintenance (as of Oct 2026) is £1,529/month in London and £1,171/month outside London, for up to 9 months, held for 28 days.
- **Loan-funded students.** A sanction letter from an Indian bank or RBI-regulated NBFC education-loan scheme can be the funds evidence. It must be dated within 6 months of the application, addressed to the student, state the amount, and have no release condition other than the visa ([Appendix Finance FIN 8.3(c), 9.2](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/immigration-rules/immigration-rules-appendix-finance); section 4).
- **Dependants.** Taught master's students who started on or after 1 January 2024 cannot bring dependants. Only PhD and research-degree students and government-sponsored students can ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/family-members)). Many Indian PG families planned around dependants, so make this clear.
- **Credibility interviews.** These are video interviews, usually with a UK-based caseworker, covering course knowledge, finances and plans, conducted in English ([University of Hertfordshire guide](https://www.herts.ac.uk/__data/assets/pdf_file/0009/428382/Credibility-Interviews-Guide-for-Students.pdf)).
- **University behaviour.** From 1 June 2026, sponsors must keep visa refusals below 5% under the tightened BCA ([Free Movement](https://freemovement.org.uk/?p=240609)). ICEF reports universities are cautious about India, and that some Indian states with low approval rates could be affected ([ICEF Monitor, May 2026](https://monitor.icef.com/?p=47591)). Expect pre-CAS interviews and stricter finance checks. ICEF named no state or university, and I found no published UK state-level pause for India (*not verified*). Confirm with the university's agent manager.
- **Post-study.** The Graduate route falls from 2 years to **18 months** for applications made from 1 January 2027. PhD graduates are unchanged ([University of Leeds](https://students.leeds.ac.uk/visa-information-after-studies/doc/graduate-visas); [ICEF](https://monitor.icef.com/2025/10/uk-to-implement-reduced-graduate-route-from-january-2027/)).

**Canada**
- **SDS ended** on 8 November 2024 ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/canada-ends-fast-track-study-permit-processing/)). All Indian applicants now use the regular stream.
- **Caps (as of Oct 2026).** The 2026 target is 408,000 study permits, of which 155,000 are for new arrivals. Master's and doctoral students at public institutions no longer need a PAL/TAL from 1 January 2026 ([Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/canada-2026-international-student-cap-and-allocations-announced.html)).
- **Approval patterns.** IRCC told Parliament the refusal rate for new permits was 59% in January–September 2025 ([IRCC CIMM briefing](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/transparency/committees/cimm-dec-04-2025/international-students.html)). Reuters-based reporting put the refusal rate for Indian post-secondary applicants at about 74% in August 2025 ([Business Standard](https://www.business-standard.com/immigration/canada-rejects-74-of-indian-student-visas-amid-tough-fraud-crackdown-125110400262_1.html)). One analysis put full-year 2025 approvals for Indian new arrivals at about 25–27%, with colleges much lower than universities ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/has-canada-reached-a-turning-point-in-study-permit-approvals/)).
- **Funds.** CAD 23,448 a year for a single applicant outside Quebec, plus tuition and travel, for applications from 1 September 2026 ([IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/financial-support.html)).
- **Advice.** For PR-motivated Punjab and Haryana college applicants, Canada is now a high-risk, low-approval route. Discuss alternatives honestly and do not promise outcomes.

**United States**
- **Interview and 214(b).** F-1 applicants must show non-immigrant intent and ties. Since 2 September 2025 almost all applicants need an in-person interview. Since 6 September 2025 applicants must apply in their country of nationality or residence ([Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/9/new-home-country-only-visa-interview-rule-and-termination-of-the-interview-waiver)). Since June 2025, F/M/J applicants must set their social-media profiles to public for vetting ([Wolfsdorf](https://wolfsdorf.com/expanded-screening-and-vetting-for-visa-applicants/)).
- **Trend.** F-1 issuances to Indians fell sharply in 2025. ICEF, citing State Department data, reports about a 57% fall for India across student visas ([ICEF Monitor, Aug 2026](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/08/us-department-of-state-issued-23-fewer-study-visas-to-international-students-in-2025/)). New international enrolments fell 17% in fall 2025 ([IIE](https://www.iie.org/news/open-doors-2025-press-release/)).
- **Popular routes.** STEM MS (OPT and the STEM extension), especially for AP and Telangana students. Steer students away from unaccredited or low-quality institutions.

**New Zealand**
- Fee-paying students must show NZD 20,000 a year for living costs. Work rights are up to 25 hours a week during study ([Immigration NZ](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/fee-paying-student-visa/)). NZ is announcing post-study changes, including a short-term graduate work visa of up to 6 months for some level 5–7 graduates ([Business Today, Mar 2026](https://www.businesstoday.in/nri/visa/story/new-zealand-introduces-6-month-graduate-visa-new-post-study-rules-explained-for-indians-522039-2026-03-24), secondary). Check INZ before advising.

**Germany (brief).** APS certificate required. A blocked account is normally required. Consultancies report €11,904 a year for 2026 ([ApplyBoard](https://assist.applyboard.com/hc/en-us/articles/32833954417933-Germany-Blocked-Account-2026-Amount-How-to-Open-One)), so confirm with the [German Missions in India](https://india.diplo.de/). Many public-university programmes need German, and English-taught places are competitive.

**Ireland (brief).** A popular alternative for Indian PG students (one-year master's, Stamp 1G post-study). Check current rules at [Irish Immigration Service](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/).

## 7. Counselling do's & don'ts

**Fraud and integrity risks seen in this market**
- **Fake admission letters.** Jalandhar-based agent Brijesh Mishra was sentenced in Canada in 2024 to three years for producing fraudulent college letters and suspect financial documents. Students from 2017–2020 later faced removal proceedings ([The PIE News](https://thepienews.com/canada-admission-scam-agent-sentenced-to-three-years-in-jail)). IRCC now verifies every LOA with the institution ([IRCC CIMM briefing](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/transparency/committees/cimm-dec-04-2025/international-students.html)).
- **Document fraud.** Fake marksheets, manufactured work experience, CA certificates and fake bank statements. In 2023 US authorities turned away 21 Indian students, mostly from the Telugu states, reportedly over document discrepancies ([Careers360](https://news.careers360.com/study-abroad-80000-telugu-students-us-visa-immigration-deported-consultant-overseas-education-andhra-pradesh-telangana/amp)).
- **Agent malpractice.** Course-hopping inducements and "guaranteed visa" claims. Australia now bans providers from paying agents commission for onshore transfers between providers. The National Code amendment took effect from 2026, and accepted-by 31 March 2026 cases are transitional ([education.gov.au](https://www.education.gov.au/esos-framework/changes-legislative-framework-overseas-students); [ICEF](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/01/australia-introduces-new-rules-restricting-agent-commissions-for-onshore-student-transfers/)).

**Regulation of education agents in India**
- **No national licensing law for study-abroad agents.** The Emigration Act 1983 covers recruitment for employment, not study ([MEA, "Emigration and You"](https://www.mea.gov.in/Images/attach/Emigration_and_You_new.pdf)). MEA's draft **Overseas Mobility (Facilitation and Welfare) Bill, 2025**, which would replace it, is framed around overseas employment ([MEA](https://www.mea.gov.in/overseasmobilitybill2025.htm)). Critics say students fall outside it ([Migrant Forum in Asia](https://mfasia.org/mfanewsletter/4968)). As of Oct 2026 I could not confirm whether it has been enacted.
- **Punjab** licenses consultants and IELTS centres under the [Punjab Travel Professionals (Regulation) Act](https://www.indiacode.nic.in/bitstream/123456789/22439/1/the_punjab_travel_professionals_regulation_act.pdf) and has revoked licences at scale, for example 263 in one district ([The Tribune](https://m.tribuneindia.com/news/jalandhar/licences-of-263-immigration-firms-ielts-centres-revoked-486964)). Expert offices operating in Punjab must display their licence number on advertising.

**Do's**
- Do take the full academic history first (Class 10, Class 12, every semester, backlogs, gaps) and match the course to it.
- Do budget the full cost with parents, including TCS and the loan margin, before applying.
- Do run the destination's official checklist (AU Document Checklist Tool, IRCC, GOV.UK) for every file.
- Do keep evidence for every claim in the SOP or GS answers.

**Don'ts**
- Don't accept "arranged" funds, CA net-worth letters without documents, or third-party sponsors with no relationship evidence.
- Don't promise PR, visa outcomes or post-study jobs.
- Don't apply to a provider just because it has a high commission or low fees when its refusal or attrition record makes the visa risky.
- Don't ignore state-level provider restrictions.

**Seasonal calendar**
- **February–April:** board exams. **April–June:** Class 10 and Class 12 results (CBSE Class 12 reported 13 May in 2026). University final-year results run May–August. Many September/October intakes get conditional offers and then final transcripts.
- **Intakes.** UK and Ireland September with January secondary. Australia February and July. Canada September and January. US fall (Aug/Sep) and spring (Jan). Germany winter (Oct) and summer (Apr).
- **Festivals.** Diwali (Oct–Nov) and wedding season (Nov–Feb) slow family decisions and paperwork. Holi (Mar), Eid, Onam (Kerala, Aug–Sep), Pongal/Sankranti (Jan) and Baisakhi (Punjab, Apr) are regional peaks. Plan bank and PCC steps around them.
- **Financial year.** LRS limits and TCS thresholds reset on 1 April, which matters when timing large fee remittances.

**Crisis and wellbeing helplines** (checked 9 Oct 2026):
- **Tele-MANAS**, the national tele-mental-health helpline run by the Ministry of Health and Family Welfare: **14416** or toll-free **1-800-891-4416** ([telemanas.mohfw.gov.in](https://telemanas.mohfw.gov.in/home); both numbers appear in the site's own contact data). Give it to a student or parent in distress who is in India.
- **KIRAN (1800-599-0019).** It was an earlier government mental-health helpline. Reports say it was folded into Tele-MANAS, but I could not confirm its current status from an official source (*not verified*), so give Tele-MANAS instead.
- **Emergencies:** India's all-in-one emergency number is **112** (ERSS). This was not re-checked on an official page for this update. A student already abroad should use the destination's emergency number and the university's student-support line.
- These numbers are for the student and family. A counsellor who sees a risk to someone's life should also tell their manager straight away.

**Communication note.** Use plain, culturally neutral language in messages and calls. Avoid religious idioms and blessings ("God willing", festival greetings tied to one faith) unless the family uses them first, and then mirror their wording only. Indian families span many faiths and languages, so do not assume them from a surname or home state.

## 8. What Edge should check/ask first

1. Student's goal: settlement/PR, degree-and-return, work after study, or prestige? Who in the family is driving it?
2. Home state and city, and whether any target provider restricts or screens that state.
3. Full academic record: board, Class 10 and 12 marks, degree length (3 or 4 years), university NAAC grade, CGPA scale, backlogs, and gaps with evidence.
4. English test status, or whether the provider accepts MOI (and whether the visa does).
5. Sponsor(s), relationship, and source and documentation of funds. Are the funds already held and for how long, or is a loan needed? Is the loan sanctioned?
6. Prior visa refusals for any country, including the details given in those applications.
7. Passport validity and name and date-of-birth consistency across documents.
8. Destination shortlist checked against current rules: AU evidence level and provider priority, UK dependants and Graduate route, Canada cap and PAL, US interview and social-media rules.
9. Timeline against result dates, intake deadlines, PCC and medical lead times, and LRS/TCS timing.
10. Preferred language and channel for parent communication.
