# Source market: Vietnam

> Counselling analysis for Expert Education & Visa Services counsellors and for Edge. Researched October 2026. Anything marked "as of" can change. Check the linked official source before quoting it to a student. Where a claim rests only on press, law-firm or consultancy reporting, the text says so.

## 1. Student profile & goals

**Scale and direction.** Vietnam's Ministry of Education and Training (MOET) reported almost 250,000 Vietnamese studying abroad in September 2025, from secondary to postgraduate level and a record high. About 4,000 hold state-budget scholarships and the rest are self-funded or on other scholarships. The top destinations were South Korea (43,300), Japan (36,300), Australia (33,000), the US (31,300), Taiwan (27,400), China (23,500) and Canada (17,100) ([Government News](https://en.baochinhphu.vn/number-of-vietnamese-students-studying-abroad-sets-record-high-111250920091952312.htm), as of Sep 2025). **Asian destinations dominate.** ICEF notes that South Korea, Taiwan and Japan together host far more Vietnamese students than all English-speaking destinations combined ([ICEF Monitor, Sep 2026](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/09/vietnam-a-strong-economy-at-home-is-powering-surging-demand-for-study-abroad/)). Expert competes with cheaper, closer options, so price and outcomes have to be addressed directly.

**Recent destination trends (as of 2026, per ICEF):**
- **Australia:** Vietnamese enrolments were down 10% (May 2026 vs May 2025). About two-thirds are in degree programmes.
- **United States:** Vietnam is the 5th-largest sender. 55% of US institutions named Vietnam a target undergraduate market.
- **United Kingdom:** Fewer than 4,000 Vietnamese students were at UK universities in 2024/25, but 13,285 were in UK–Vietnam transnational (TNE) programmes.
- **New Zealand:** Vietnamese enrolments rose 11% in 2025.
- **South Korea:** Shifting from language study to degrees. About 60% of Vietnamese students there were in degree programmes in April 2026, up from 35% in 2019.

All figures are from [ICEF Monitor, Sep 2026](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/09/vietnam-a-strong-economy-at-home-is-powering-surging-demand-for-study-abroad/).

**Motivations.**
- **Degree-and-career, price-sensitive.** The main family worry is affording study and living costs. Work prospects after graduation and the chance to work while studying matter to families ([ICEF market snapshot, 2024](https://monitor.icef.com/2024/03/market-snapshot-a-guide-to-international-student-recruitment-in-vietnam/)).
- **Scholarship-driven.** Wealthier families will go without scholarships. Lower-income families depend on them, and middle-class students prefer scholarships from high-ranking universities (same source).
- **Prestige and return.** The economy is strong and domestic options (international schools, TNE, branch campuses) are growing, so for many families the "degree-and-return" plan is credible ([ICEF Monitor, Sep 2026](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/09/vietnam-a-strong-economy-at-home-is-powering-surging-demand-for-study-abroad/)). Settlement is a goal for some students, especially those heading to Australia, Canada and the US. Treat it as a segment, not the default.
- **Family business heirs** (counsellor observation, not a sourced statistic): children of owners of household businesses and SMEs often study business, finance or hospitality intending to return. Their funding evidence is often informal (see section 4).

**Levels and fields.** Most degree-seekers still go abroad for undergraduate study, but more are waiting until master's level. Popular fields are tourism/hospitality, STEM, business and health. US K-12 enrolment from Vietnam has been falling ([ICEF, 2024](https://monitor.icef.com/2024/03/market-snapshot-a-guide-to-international-student-recruitment-in-vietnam/)). Japan remains largely a language-school destination ([ICEF, Sep 2026](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/09/vietnam-a-strong-economy-at-home-is-powering-surging-demand-for-study-abroad/)).

**Hanoi vs Ho Chi Minh City.** British Council research summarised by ICEF describes Hanoi families as more conservative and harder to persuade. They weigh ranking and prestige, and word of mouth matters more. Scholarships and fee discounts work better in Hanoi. HCMC (Saigon) families are described as more open-minded and focused on practical benefits. In the south, location and financial aid packages are key drivers ([ICEF, 2024](https://monitor.icef.com/2024/03/market-snapshot-a-guide-to-international-student-recruitment-in-vietnam/)). Da Nang and the central provinces are a smaller but growing segment. Australia and the UK both have visa application centres there (section 5).

**Government-sponsored students.** State-budget scholarship holders fall under Decree 86/2021/ND-CP. It sets reporting duties and extension rules, and requires students to repay training costs if they don't complete the programme or don't follow their post-graduation work assignment ([LawNet summary](https://lawnet.vn/thong-tin-phap-luat/en/thong-bao-van-ban-moi/decree-on-vietnamese-citizens-going-abroad-to-study-104562.html); [LawNet, repayment](https://lawnet.vn/giao-duc/en/when-do-international-students-under-state-budget-scholarship-of-vietnam-have-to-return-training-cos-3888.html)). **Decree 322/2026** reportedly amends Decree 86/2021's study-abroad rules, in force from 5 Oct 2026. Its content and effect on state-sponsored students are **not verified**, so check the current text with MOET before advising on reporting or repayment duties. Edge should flag these students. Their study plan and return obligation are fixed by the sponsor, which also helps genuine-student assessments.

## 2. Family & decision-making

- **Parents decide and pay.** In an INTO survey cited by ICEF, more than 4 in 5 Vietnamese students said parents were key influencers. Parents shape destination, institution and field ([ICEF, 2024](https://monitor.icef.com/2024/03/market-snapshot-a-guide-to-international-student-recruitment-in-vietnam/)). Funding is usually parental, sometimes with grandparents or relatives abroad.
- **Face-to-face matters.** 92% of surveyed students rated face-to-face contact with university representatives and agents as very important (same source). Offer office or video meetings with both parents for key decisions: destination, budget, loan, and the visa risk explanation.
- **Channels.** Vietnamese parents are highly engaged on Facebook (same source). **Zalo is the default messaging app for families.** It had 81.3 million monthly users and 2.2 billion messages a day in H1 2026 ([VnExpress, 3 Aug 2026](https://vnexpress.net/zalo-dat-hon-81-trieu-nguoi-dung-moi-thang-5104513.html)). Decision Lab found 88% of Vietnam's online population used it, 96% of Gen X (the parents' generation), and 48% named it an app they "cannot live without" ([Decision Lab, Q4 2021](https://www.decisionlab.co/blog/here-comes-zalo)). Expect parents to want Zalo calls, voice notes and group chats rather than email. Send written summaries after calls, because parents often share them with the wider family.
- **Zalo record-keeping (practice note).** Zalo chats live on personal phones, outside Expert's systems. Log every key piece of advice, fee quote and decision in the CRM. Don't let Zalo be the only record. Don't ask families to send passports, bank books or judicial records as plain Zalo photos. Use Expert's secure upload channel. Zalo's end-to-end encryption launched as an opt-in beta in 2022 ([Zalo Help](https://help.zalo.me/huong-dan/tinh-nang-co-ban/ma-hoa-dau-cuoi-nang-cap-bao-mat-tin-nhan/)), and whether it is still available is not verified.
- **Tone and language.** Write parent-facing drafts in **Vietnamese** with appropriate forms of address (e.g. "Kính gửi Quý phụ huynh", "anh/chị"). Student-facing drafts can be in English if the student is comfortable. Keep document names in both languages, for example "học bạ (transcript book)", "sổ tiết kiệm (savings book)", "phiếu lý lịch tư pháp (judicial record certificate)", so families can collect the right papers.
- **Expectations to manage.** Families compare total cost with Korea, Japan and Taiwan. Present the full cost, scholarship odds and work-rights limits honestly.

## 3. Academic background

**School system.** 5 years primary, 4 years lower secondary and 3 years upper secondary (grades 10–12, ages 15–18). The school-leaving credential is the **Bằng tốt nghiệp trung học phổ thông** (upper secondary graduation diploma), obtained by passing the national graduation exam ([Nuffic](https://www.nuffic.nl/en/education-systems/vietnam/primary-and-secondary-education)).
- **Exam format.** From 2025 the exam has four subjects: compulsory Literature and Mathematics plus two chosen subjects. Before 2025 there were six (same source). A foreign language is no longer compulsory in the exam, so English evidence must come from IELTS/TOEFL/PTE or school results.
- **Timing (as of 2026).** The 2026 exam ran on 11–12 June with about 1.22 million registered candidates, and scores were released on 1 July 2026 ([VOV](https://english.vov.vn/en/society/students-ready-for-nationwide-high-school-graduation-exam-2026-post1305301.vov); [Bao Tay Ninh](https://news.baotayninh.vn/over-1-22-million-students-register-for-2026-national-high-school-graduation-exam-146055.html); [Lao Dong schedule](https://news.laodong.vn/giao-duc/lich-cong-bo-diem-thi-tot-nghiep-thpt-nam-2026-1724833.ldo)). Students usually get a **provisional graduation certificate (Giấy chứng nhận tốt nghiệp)** first. The official diploma normally follows within a year, after which the provisional certificate is no longer valid ([Nuffic](https://www.nuffic.nl/en/node/261)).
- **Học bạ** (the school record book) holds grades 10–12 results by year and is the transcript most destinations want.

**Grading.** Schools and universities use a **10-point scale**. 4–5 and below 4 are fails, and bands run from Xuất sắc (outstanding) down to Kém (poor) ([Nuffic](https://www.nuffic.nl/en/node/261)). For credit-based university programmes, Circular 08/2021/TT-BGDĐT converts course marks to letters (A 8.5–10, B 7.0–8.4, C 5.5–6.9, D 4.0–5.4, F below 4.0) and to a **4-point GPA**. Classification bands on the 4-point scale are: Xuất sắc 3.6–4.0, Giỏi 3.2–<3.6, Khá 2.5–<3.2, Trung bình 2.0–<2.5 ([LawNet summary of Circular 08/2021](https://lawnet.vn/thong-tin-phap-luat/en/giao-duc/regulations-on-academic-performance-assessment-of-universities-in-vietnam-105871.html)). Universities can set their own letter tables (e.g. [HUST](https://www.hust.edu.vn/en/academics/savefile/academic-information/grading-system-554906.html)), so convert using the scale printed on the transcript.

**Degrees.** Bachelor's degrees (cử nhân, kỹ sư) usually take 4 years, sometimes 5 for agricultural and technical programmes. Master's degrees take 2 years ([Nuffic](https://www.nuffic.nl/en/education-systems/vietnam/higher-education)). Unlike Indian 3-year degrees, Vietnamese bachelor's degrees rarely raise a length problem for master's entry.

**Credential evaluation and MOI.** Evaluators such as WES want the transcript (bảng điểm) and diploma with certified English translations. I could not find a WES page listing Vietnam-specific requirements, so confirm with WES before sending. Germany requires an **APS certificate** for applicants whose school or university qualifications are from Vietnam ([Expatrio](https://www.expatrio.com/about-germany/aps-certificate), secondary). Many universities in Vietnam teach in Vietnamese, so MOI letters only help graduates of English-medium programmes (international programmes, TNE, branch campuses). Most providers will ask for an English test.

## 4. Money

**Foreign-exchange rules (State Bank of Vietnam).** Under **Circular 20/2022/TT-NHNN** (effective 15 February 2023), Vietnamese residents can buy, transfer and carry foreign currency for study abroad through licensed banks. The amount is based on the costs in the foreign institution's notice (tuition, living costs). Funds can come from the family's own foreign-currency deposits or from currency bought from authorised banks ([LuatVietnam](https://english.luatvietnam.vn/legal-updates/levels-of-foreign-currencies-bought-transferred-and-carried-abroad-892-92780-article.html); [text of Circular 20/2022](https://dulieuphapluat.vn/van-ban/ngan-hang-tien-te/thong-tu-202022tt-nhnn-cua-ngan-hang-nha-nuoc-viet-nam-ve-viec-huong-dan-hoat-dong-chuyen-tien-mot-chieu-tu-viet-nam-ra-nuoc-ngoai-va-thanh-toan-chuyen-tien-cho-cac-giao-dich-vang-lai-khac-cua-nguoi-cu-tru-la-to-chuc-ca-nhan-1100281.html)). Banks add their own limits. For example, HSBC Vietnam caps transfers by the destination country's per-capita income ([HSBC Vietnam FAQ](https://www.hsbc.com.vn/en-vn/accounts/faq/)). For counsellors this means the **offer letter or CoE fee breakdown is the remittance document**, so make sure it states both tuition and living costs.

**Education loans.**
- Vietnamese banks offer overseas-study loans. BIDV advertises loans up to 80% of total cost over up to 10 years ([BIDV](https://www.bidv.com.vn/bidv_en/bidv-blog/tin-dung/nhung-luu-y-quan-trong-khi-vay-du-hoc), undated). Techcombank lends up to 85% of course costs and offers balance confirmation and **loan commitment letters** for proof of funds ([Techcombank](https://techcombank.com/thong-tin/thong-bao/vay-du-hoc-va-chung-minh-nang-luc-tai-chinh)). Loans are usually secured on property (land-use certificates) or savings.
- **Borrowing to create a savings balance is restricted.** From 1 September 2023, credit institutions may not lend "to deposit money" (để gửi tiền) under Circular 06/2023/TT-NHNN. That clause was not among those suspended by Circular 10/2023 ([LuatVietnam](https://luatvietnam.vn/tin-van-ban-moi/ngung-thi-hanh-3-truong-hop-cam-cho-vay-tu-01-9-2023-186-95309-article.html)). This targets the old "borrow to open a savings book for the visa" practice. Press reporting has long described such loans as a cosmetic service that some borrowers used to mislead visa officers ([VnEconomy](https://vneconomy.vn/vay-von-du-hoc-mot-dich-vu-doi-pho.htm)).
- The state Social Policy Bank student loan is for domestic study, not study abroad (secondary reporting). I did not verify this on the bank's own site.

**Proof-of-funds practice and red flags.**
- Common evidence includes a **savings book (sổ tiết kiệm)** with a bank balance confirmation, payment-account statements, sponsor income evidence (labour contracts, salary statements, personal income-tax records), household-business registration and tax records, and property ownership as supporting evidence of wealth (not liquid funds).
- **Red flags** (counsellor guidance drawn from the sources above): savings books opened or topped up just before the application, with no history; household-business income with no tax filings; land or gold offered as "funds"; balance confirmations from banks that cannot be verified; and sponsors with no documented relationship. Destinations hold funds differently. The UK requires 28 consecutive days ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money)), and Canada asks for 6 months of statements showing the source ([IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/financial-support.html)).
- **Currency.** Fees are in foreign currency and VND moves against it, so build a buffer and show families the conversion assumptions.

## 6. Destination-by-destination guidance

**Australia**
- **Fees.** From 1 July 2026 the student visa charge rose to AUD 2,500 for most applicants, but **ASEAN citizens, including Vietnamese, pay AUD 2,050** ([VOV](https://kynguyenvuonminh.vov.vn/en/society/australia-maintains-lower-student-visa-fees-for-vietnamese-and-asean-applicants-post1312104.vov)). That is an advantage over India and South Asia.
- **Evidence level.** Home Affairs does not publish country levels. Consultancy reporting placed Vietnam at Evidence Level 2 in 2025 ([Onederland](https://onederland.com.au/australia-student-visa-evidence-levels-update/)). Unlike India, Vietnam was not among the countries moved to EL3 in January 2026. I could not verify its level after the reported 27 March 2026 revision. Always run the [Document Checklist Tool](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/web-evidentiary-tool) with the CRICOS provider.
- **Processing.** MD115 (from 14 Nov 2025) prioritises applications by how far the provider is through its allocation ([Study Australia](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/Agent-Hub/agent-news-index/ministerial-direction-115)). The Genuine Student requirement applies.
- **Pathways.** Foundation and diploma to bachelor's, master's after a Vietnamese 4-year bachelor's, and TNE articulation (e.g. RMIT Vietnam, which enrols over 15,000 students in-country per [ICEF](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/09/vietnam-a-strong-economy-at-home-is-powering-surging-demand-for-study-abroad/)). Credit transfer from TNE strengthens a genuine-student case.
- **Common refusal risks** (general GS factors): weak course progression logic, funds that don't hold up, and an unexplained choice of a low-cost provider.

**United Kingdom**
- **Vietnam is not on the differential-evidence (low-risk) list, although Cambodia, Indonesia, Malaysia and Thailand are** ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/money)). Vietnamese applicants must therefore submit full financial and academic evidence. Maintenance is £1,529/month in London and £1,171/month outside, for up to 9 months, held for 28 days (as of Oct 2026).
- **No Vietnam-specific restriction found.** The UK's March 2026 study-visa refusals target Afghanistan, Cameroon, Myanmar and Sudan ([GOV.UK news](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/crackdown-on-legal-visa-abuse-stems-surge-in-asylum-claims)). Universities must still keep refusals below 5% under the BCA from 1 June 2026 ([Free Movement](https://freemovement.org.uk/?p=240609)), so expect pre-CAS checks and possible credibility interviews.
- **Dependants** only for PhD and research-degree or government-sponsored students ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/student-visa/family-members)). The **Graduate route** falls to 18 months for applications from 1 Jan 2027 ([University of Leeds](https://students.leeds.ac.uk/visa-information-after-studies/doc/graduate-visas)).
- **Fit.** One-year master's and TNE top-ups suit degree-and-return families who care about cost and time. Few Vietnamese students are at UK universities onshore (under 4,000 in 2024/25), compared with large TNE enrolment ([ICEF](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/09/vietnam-a-strong-economy-at-home-is-powering-surging-demand-for-study-abroad/)).

**Canada**
- Vietnam was one of the SDS countries. **SDS ended on 8 November 2024** ([EIG Law](https://eiglaw.com/ircc-ends-the-student-direct-stream-and-nigeria-student-express/)), so Vietnamese applicants now use the regular stream.
- **Caps and funds.** The 2026 target is 155,000 new-arrival permits ([Fragomen](https://www.fragomen.com/insights/canada-2026-international-student-cap-and-allocations-announced.html)). Proof of funds is CAD 23,448/year (single, outside Quebec) for applications from 1 Sep 2026 ([IRCC](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/study-canada/study-permit/get-documents/financial-support.html)). The overall new-permit refusal rate was 59% in Jan–Sep 2025 ([IRCC CIMM](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/corporate/transparency/committees/cimm-dec-04-2025/international-students.html)). **I could not find a published Vietnam-specific approval rate.**
- **Police certificate (if IRCC requests one):** Judicial Record Certificate **No.2** ([IRCC, Vietnam](https://www.canada.ca/en/immigration-refugees-citizenship/services/application/medical-police/police-certificates/how/vietnam.html)). It must be the paper version, because the VNeID e-certificate is domestic-only (section 5). Canada did not object to Vietnam's apostille accession ([HCCH](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/notifications/?csid=1557&disp=type), as of 9 Oct 2026).

**United States**
- **Volume.** Vietnam reached its highest US total in 2024/25 ([IIE](https://www.iie.org/news/open-doors-2025-press-release/)). Reported counts differ: 25,584 per one Vietnamese outlet citing IIE ([Bao Bac Ninh](https://en.baobacninhtv.vn/vietnamese-student-number-in-us-reaches-all-time-high-postid431691.bbg)) and about 31,300 per MOET ([Government News](https://en.baochinhphu.vn/number-of-vietnamese-students-studying-abroad-sets-record-high-111250920091952312.htm)). Use the [Open Doors data](https://opendoorsdata.org/) for exact figures.
- **Interview and 214(b).** In-person interviews are required for almost all applicants (since Sep 2025). Applicants must apply in their country of nationality or residence ([Faegre Drinker](https://www.faegredrinker.com/en/insights/publications/2025/9/new-home-country-only-visa-interview-rule-and-termination-of-the-interview-waiver)). Social-media profiles must be public for F/M/J ([Wolfsdorf](https://wolfsdorf.com/expanded-screening-and-vetting-for-visa-applicants/)). Vietnam is **not** covered by the January 2026 travel-ban proclamation ([Harvard International Office](https://www.hio.harvard.edu/news/2025/12/update-expanded-travel-restrictions)).
- **Popular routes.** Community college 2+2 transfer, direct UG, and STEM master's with OPT. Interviews are in English, so rehearse course and return-plan answers truthfully.

**New Zealand.** Vietnamese enrolments grew 11% in 2025 ([ICEF](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/09/vietnam-a-strong-economy-at-home-is-powering-surging-demand-for-study-abroad/)). Living-cost funds are NZD 20,000/year. Work rights are up to 25 hours/week during study ([Immigration NZ](https://www.immigration.govt.nz/visas/fee-paying-student-visa/)).

**Germany (brief).** APS certificate required for Vietnamese qualifications. The APS office at the German Embassy Hanoi checks admission eligibility and verifies Vietnamese diplomas and results, and has issued digital certificates since 1 Nov 2023 ([German Embassy Hanoi, APS](https://vietnam.diplo.de/vn-vi/%C4%91%E1%BB%A9c-vi%E1%BB%87tnam/2546866-2546866)). Confirm the APS and blocked-account rules with the [German Embassy Hanoi](https://vietnam.diplo.de/).
- **No apostille for Germany.** Germany objected to Vietnam's accession on 20 May 2026 ([HCCH](https://www.hcch.net/en/instruments/conventions/status-table/notifications/?csid=1557&disp=type)). Vietnamese civil documents (birth, marriage, judicial record) for Germany therefore still go through MOFA consular certification and then German Embassy legalisation or verification. Check the embassy's [consular services](https://vietnam.diplo.de/vn-vi/dichvulanhsu/1223654-1223654) for the current procedure. Austria and Czechia objected too (same source).

**Ireland (brief).** A smaller niche. Check [Irish Immigration Service](https://www.irishimmigration.ie/) for the current student and Stamp 1G rules.

## 7. Counselling do's & don'ts

**Fraud and integrity risks seen in this market**
- **Japan** banned 12 Vietnamese agents from submitting visa applications over forgery concerns ([The PIE News, tag archive](https://thepienews.com/tags/fraud/page/3/)). Japan's ambassador publicly warned students about brokers claiming embassy connections ([VietNamNet](https://vietnamnet.vn/en/japanese-ambassador-issues-student-crime-warning-E216738.html)). Expect these patterns to show up in Anglophone applications too.
- **Proof-of-funds manipulation.** This means borrowed or "rented" savings balances. Bank lending "to deposit money" has been barred since September 2023 (section 4).
- **Scams against families.** Da Nang police warned about a fake university "exchange" offer that asked families to deposit money to prove funds ([Bao Nghe An](https://baonghean.vn/en/print/10296916.html)).

**Regulation of education agents in Vietnam (status uncertain, as of 9 Oct 2026).** From 20 November 2024, **Decree 125/2024/ND-CP** treated study-abroad consultancy as a **conditional business**. It required a registration certificate from the provincial **Department of Education and Training (DOET)**, valid for 5 years. Conditions included proper premises and information resources, and consultants with a university degree, foreign-language proficiency at level 4 or above on the 6-level framework, and a study-abroad consultancy training certificate ([KPMG Vietnam](https://kpmg.com/vn/en/home/insights/2024/11/legal-update-decree-124-and-125-updates.html); [Apolat Legal](https://apolatlegal.com/study-abroad-consultancy-and-important-adjustments-from-november-20-2024/)). A 2026 change reportedly removed the business-licence and DOET-registration requirement from 1 July 2026. That is **not verified**: no MOET, government-portal or legal-database source could be retrieved on 9 Oct 2026, so check with MOET/DOET. Until it is confirmed, Expert's Vietnam offices should keep their DOET certificate (or confirm in writing that it is no longer required). Edge should not state either way that a registration is or is not required, and should not imply that unregistered sub-agents may advise.

**Do's**
- Do run a full-cost comparison (including Korea, Japan and Taiwan alternatives when the family raises them) and present scholarships realistically.
- Do get parents into at least one decision meeting and send the summary in Vietnamese.
- Do check the provisional vs official diploma status and the học bạ for all three upper-secondary years.
- Do keep a funds history (statements, tax records), not just a balance on a single date.
- Do verify each destination's current stance on Vietnamese apostilles (new since Sep 2026). Germany, Austria and Czechia objected, so they still need legalisation (section 5).
- Do log Zalo advice, quotes and decisions in the CRM, and collect identity and financial documents through Expert's secure channel, not as chat photos.

**Don'ts**
- Don't arrange or suggest loans to "show" funds, or third-party balance services.
- Don't use the electronic VNeID judicial record abroad. Request the paper certificate and legalise or apostille it.
- Don't promise settlement. Don't reuse templated GS or SOP text, because officers notice repeated wording across agents.
- Don't ignore Decree 86/2021 obligations for state-scholarship students (as amended by Decree 322/2026 from 5 Oct 2026, per reports; the changes are not verified).

**Seasonal calendar**
- **Tết (Lunar New Year, late January–February):** offices, banks and agencies slow down for 1–2 weeks. Families make big decisions before or after it.
- **June:** national graduation exam (11–12 June in 2026). **1 July:** scores. **July–August:** domestic university admissions round (registration 2–14 July and results around 9 August in 2026, per schedule reports). Overseas UG offers for September intakes often depend on these results.
- **Mid-Autumn Festival (Sept/Oct)** is a family occasion, and back-to-school in early September.
- **Intakes:** Australia Feb/Jul, UK Sep/Jan, US Aug/Jan, Canada Sep/Jan, NZ Feb/Jul. Korea and Japan intakes (Mar/Apr, Sep/Oct) compete for the same students.

**Crisis and wellbeing helplines** (checked 9 Oct 2026). If a student or family member shows distress or mentions self-harm, give these numbers and escalate to the branch manager. Counsellors are not clinicians.
- **115**: emergency medical services, nationwide ([Find A Helpline, Vietnam](https://findahelpline.com/countries/vn)).
- **111**: National Child Protection Helpline. Free, 24/7, for children (under 16) and adults reporting abuse, violence, trafficking or hardship ([tongdai111.vn](https://tongdai111.vn/)). Its site describes it as run under the former labour ministry's Department of Children. Which ministry runs it after the 2025 restructuring is not verified.
- **No national adult mental-health hotline** could be verified on a government source. NGO lines listed by [Find A Helpline](https://findahelpline.com/countries/vn): **Ngày Mai** peer-support line, 096 306 1414; **HOPE** suicide-prevention line, 0865 044 400; **CSAGA** gender-based-violence line, 024 3333 5599; **Peace House Shelter**, 1900 969 680 (24/7). Hours for Ngày Mai and HOPE are not stated. Ngày Mai's own site could not be reached, so its number is not verified at source.
- Onshore students should use the destination's services and their university's counselling team.

**Communication note.** Keep language culturally and religiously neutral. Families' beliefs vary and many are not religious, so don't use religious idioms or blessings ("God willing", "pray for the visa") unless the family uses them first. Use respectful forms of address (anh/chị, Quý phụ huynh), and avoid luck or fate framing for visa outcomes.

## 8. What Edge should check/ask first

1. Student's goal (degree-and-return, work after study, settlement), whether the student is state-sponsored (Decree 86/2021, reportedly amended by Decree 322/2026), and who in the family decides.
2. City or province, and whether the family is in Hanoi, HCMC, Da Nang or elsewhere (affects VAC, motivation and messaging).
3. Upper-secondary status: exam taken, provisional or official diploma, học bạ grades 10–12. For graduates: university, 10- or 4-point scale, classification, and whether the programme is English-medium or TNE.
4. English test status. Remind them the national exam no longer requires a foreign language.
5. Sponsor(s) and funds history. Are savings or business income documented over time? Is a loan sanctioned, and by which bank?
6. Prior visa applications or refusals, including Korea, Japan and Taiwan.
7. Passport validity and name consistency with and without diacritics.
8. Document route for each destination: apostille (not for Germany, Austria or Czechia, which objected) or consular legalisation, paper judicial record (No.2 for immigration use), and certified translations.
9. Timeline against exam and score dates, Tết, intake deadlines, and medical and police-record lead times.
10. Preferred language (Vietnamese for parents) and channel (Zalo, phone or Facebook).
