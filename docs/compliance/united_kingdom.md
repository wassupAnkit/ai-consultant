# United Kingdom — Compliance guide for Expert counsellors

*Researched 9 October 2026. Rules are stated as at that date, and time-sensitive points carry "as of"; anything marked "not verified" could not be confirmed against an official source and must not be told to students as fact.*

## 1. Bottom line for counsellors
<!-- keywords: can i, is this allowed, red lines, uk compliance, quick summary, what should i do, guarantee, promise visa, mislead, uk law, never coach -->

**Help students choose and apply to UK courses honestly. Never give tailored UK immigration advice unless you are authorised, never guarantee an outcome, and refer visa questions to a regulated adviser.**

- Giving immigration advice in the UK without being a "qualified person" is a crime: up to 2 years' prison and a fine ([Immigration and Asylum Act 1999 s.84](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/33/section/84), [s.91](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/33/section/91)).
- Pointing students to GOV.UK and helping with the university application is fine. Giving an opinion on one student's visa case is not ([IAA practice note, Aug 2025](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688886abbe2291b14d11b03b/IAA_Immigration_Assistance_practice_note_July_2025.pdf)).
- Never guarantee admission, scholarships or visas ([University of Staffordshire](https://www.staffs.ac.uk/international/your-application-journey/uk-agent-quality-framework-aqf) *(provider policy)*). Never give false, incomplete or misleading information ([National Code, Standard 3](https://www.aqf.info/s/The-national-code-for-UK-education-agents-July-2025.pdf)).
- As of 7 April 2026, partner universities must record Expert on each CAS it recruited for, and keep evidence of how they manage agents ([Sponsorship duties 3.24–3.31](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/student-sponsor-guidance/sponsorship-duties-accessible)).
- Every refused visa counts against the university's refusal-rate limit, which is under 5% from 1 June 2026 (§5).
- **Do instead:** give official information, flag the risk honestly, and refer the student to an Immigration Advice Authority (IAA) registered adviser or a solicitor ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/find-an-immigration-adviser)).

## 2. Who regulates immigration advice
<!-- keywords: oisc, iaa, immigration advice authority, regulated adviser, registered adviser, qualified person, level 1, level 2, level 3, section 84, section 91, criminal offence, exemption, adviser finder, oisc registration, iaa fine -->

**UK immigration advice is regulated by the Immigration Advice Authority (IAA), formerly the Office of the Immigration Services Commissioner (OISC).**

- **Name:** the OISC became the IAA on 16 January 2025. Existing OISC registrations stayed valid ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/government/news/enhanced-agenda-and-identity-for-uk-immigration-advice-regulator)).
- **What is regulated:** advice about one particular person's immigration matter, and acting for them with the Home Office or a tribunal. It covers advice given "in the United Kingdom", in the course of a business, whether the client is in the UK or abroad ([s.82](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/33/section/82)).
- **Who may advise:** IAA-registered advisers, authorised solicitors and barristers, and people under their supervision ([s.84](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/33/section/84)). Anyone else commits an offence ([s.91](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/33/section/91)).
- **Levels:** Level 1 is advice on simple applications, Level 2 adds casework (e.g. administrative review), Level 3 adds tribunal work ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/find-an-immigration-adviser/what-advisers-can-do)).
- **Exemptions:** licensed sponsors may give free advice to the students they sponsor ([SI 2022/235](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2022/235/contents/made)). Listed universities and their staff are also exempt ([SI 2001/1403](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/uksi/2001/1403/contents/made)). Whether an agent "acting on behalf of" a university is covered: **not verified**, so do not rely on it.
- **Abroad:** a firm advising from outside the UK is not IAA-regulated. It becomes regulated if it meets clients in the UK ([IAA practice note](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688886abbe2291b14d11b03b/IAA_Immigration_Assistance_practice_note_July_2025.pdf)).
- **New powers:** the Border Security, Asylum and Immigration Act 2025 lets the IAA fine unregulated advisers up to £15,000 ([IAA factsheet](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/border-security-asylum-and-immigration-bill-iaa-factsheet/border-security-asylum-and-immigration-bill-iaa-factsheet)). Whether that power is in force as of 9 Oct 2026: **not verified**.

## 3. What an unregulated adviser may and may not say
<!-- keywords: can i advise, which visa, which uk visa, switch, switching, dependants, dependant, qualify, eligible, refusal advice, reapply, extension, graduate route, interview answers, signposting, tell a student, cas steps, what can i say -->

**You may give general information and help with university applications. You may not tell an individual what their visa position is or what to do about it.**

- **You may** ([IAA practice note](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688886abbe2291b14d11b03b/IAA_Immigration_Assistance_practice_note_July_2025.pdf)):
  - share general information and point the student to GOV.UK guidance;
  - help with university applications, documents and the steps a university sets before it issues a CAS;
  - translate, and help a student follow UKVI's own instructions while the student applies;
  - help a student understand what their regulated adviser is asking for.
- **You may not, unless you are authorised** (same note; [s.82](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/33/section/82)):
  - say which visa route suits this student, or whether they qualify on funds, English or course level;
  - say how to answer an application question, or whether their documents meet the requirements;
  - advise on a refusal, an administrative review or a reapplication;
  - advise on switching visa inside the UK, extending a visa, the Graduate route, dependants or overstaying;
  - submit applications or write to the Home Office for the student.
- **Scope:** these limits apply to advice given in the UK, including from Expert's UK office (§8). Offshore staff should keep to the same line, because partner contracts and the National Code require lawful, accurate advice ([National Code 1, 3](https://www.aqf.info/s/The-national-code-for-UK-education-agents-July-2025.pdf)). That offshore point is an Expert-practice inference.
- **Say instead:** "That depends on your circumstances. Here is the GOV.UK page, and here is a registered adviser who can advise you."

## 4. UK Agent Quality Framework and the National Code
<!-- keywords: aqf, agent quality framework, national code, code of ethical practice, british council, certified counsellor, certification, uk agent and counsellor training, pledge, agent list, white paper, sponsor guidance, record agent on cas, buila, ukcisa, uuki -->

**The AQF began as a voluntary sector scheme. Since 7 April 2026 it has been part of sponsor duties for any university that uses agents.**

- **Origin:** set up in 2022 by the British Council, BUILA, UKCISA and UUKi ([UKCISA](https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/our-work/working-with-agents/)). It has four principles: student choice, good governance, ethical and competent practice, and transparency ([AQF](https://www.aqf.info/)).
- **It is not a licence:** the AQF "does not provide accreditation, licensing, or ongoing assurance" of agents ([AQF](https://www.aqf.info/)).
- **The National Code (July 2025 edition):** all counselling staff working with UK-bound students should hold the British Council "UK knowledge trained" certificate ([Code 5.2](https://www.aqf.info/s/The-national-code-for-UK-education-agents-July-2025.pdf)).
- **The certificate:** the training is free. The certificate is valid for 2 years, and the British Council database lists those who opt in ([British Council](https://www.britishcouncil.org/education/agents-counsellors/database)). The live page could not be loaded on 9 Oct 2026, so this is **not verified** against it.
- **Government:** the 12 May 2025 White Paper proposed requiring every sponsor that uses agents to sign up to the AQF ([White Paper ¶119](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/restoring-control-over-the-immigration-system-white-paper)). From 7 April 2026, sponsors must ([Sponsorship duties](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/student-sponsor-guidance/sponsorship-duties-accessible)):
  - record on the CAS the main agent behind each recruit, even a one-off (3.24–3.27);
  - commit to the AQF and keep evidence that they manage agents against it and the Code (3.30–3.31);
  - report changes to their agent list within 20 working days (7.2).
- **Universities' own rules:** some require at least one certified counsellor in each branch ([Coventry](https://www.coventry.ac.uk/partner-hub/agent-requirements/) *(provider policy)*). Some publish their approved agents ([Staffordshire](https://www.staffs.ac.uk/international/your-application-journey/uk-agent-quality-framework-aqf) *(provider policy)*).

## 5. Basic Compliance Assessment (BCA) thresholds and refusals
<!-- keywords: bca, basic compliance assessment, refusal rate, visa refusal rate, enrolment rate, completion rate, rag rating, red rating, cas allocation, pre-screening, credibility interview, paused market, weak file, sponsor licence -->

**Universities lose CAS and face action plans if too many of their students are refused. That is why they pre-screen hard.**

- **Thresholds** ([Student sponsor compliance 2.14](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/student-sponsor-guidance/student-sponsor-compliance-accessible), v06/2026):
  - for assessments applied for from 1 June 2026: refusals under 5%, enrolment at least 95%, completion at least 85%;
  - before 1 June 2026 the limits were 10%, 90% and 85%;
  - completion rises to 90% from 1 June 2027.
- **Dates:** reports of a September 2025 start ([PIE](https://thepienews.com/uk-stricter-bca-rules-to-take-effect-in-september/) *(secondary)*) are superseded by the official 1 June 2026 date above.
- **Ratings:** each university gets a Red-Amber-Green rating, set by its worst measure. Refusals of 5% or more are red, and 4% to under 5% is amber. Ratings are published on the register of student sponsors (2.28–2.38).
- **A red rating:** means an action plan of at least 12 months, a CAS cut of at least 10%, and a final warning (2.44–2.46). Universities close to a threshold can also get an action plan (4.14).
- **Withdrawn CAS:** a CAS withdrawn before the visa decision is left out of the refusal rate (2.19). That is one reason universities pause or withdraw doubtful files.
- **Agents:** UKVI weighs agents linked to past abuse when it sets CAS allocations ([Sponsorship duties 10.3](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/student-sponsor-guidance/sponsorship-duties-accessible)).
- **For counsellors:** never push a weak or doubtful file, and never script a student's answers for a credibility interview. Some universities have reportedly paused high-refusal markets (**not verified** here).

## 6. Commission and agent agreements
<!-- keywords: commission, agent agreement, agent contract, disclose commission, charge both, service fee, student pays, provider hopping, transfer commission, switching from another, another university, change university, poaching, incentive, inducement, refund policy, sub agent -->

**No UK law caps agent commission or bans commission on transfers. The National Code, provider contracts and the CAS rules set the limits.**

- **No law found:** we found no UK statute or rule on commission levels, and no ban on commission for students who move between UK providers, as of 9 Oct 2026. Australia's 2026 transfer-commission ban does not apply to the UK.
- **What the National Code requires** ([National Code, July 2025](https://www.aqf.info/s/The-national-code-for-UK-education-agents-July-2025.pdf)):
  - tell students which services the institution pays for and which they pay for. The commission amount need not be disclosed (2.2);
  - declare conflicts of interest, "especially when service fees are charged to both" the provider and the student (ethical framework; 1.2);
  - be open about your fees, and have a documented refund policy and signed student agreements (3.5, 2.5);
  - disclose marketing incentives to providers, and do not lure students away from competitors (2.3);
  - never help students move to a course at the same or a lower level to earn extra commission. If a student has sound reasons to move, tell both providers (2.9);
  - declare your sub-agents, and keep protocols for managing them (1.3).
- **On the CAS:** the agent recorded is the one the university credits with the recruitment, for example the one paid commission ([Sponsorship duties 3.27](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/student-sponsor-guidance/sponsorship-duties-accessible)). Commission disputes are settled under the agent contract, not by the student.

## 7. Complaints and escalation
<!-- keywords: complaint, complain, oia, ombudsman, completion of procedures, spso, nipso, report adviser, unregulated adviser, scam, fraud, action fraud, report fraud, immigration crime, student unhappy -->

**Students complain to the university first, then to an ombudsman. Report bad immigration advice to the IAA and report fraud to the police.**

- **Expert first:** agents must have clear complaint routes and must point students to providers' complaint processes ([National Code 2.4](https://www.aqf.info/s/The-national-code-for-UK-education-agents-July-2025.pdf)).
- **University:** the student finishes the university's own process and gets a Completion of Procedures letter ([OIA](https://www.oiahe.org.uk/students/can-you-complain-to-us/)).
- **England and Wales:** the OIA takes complaints from current and former students, within 12 months of that letter. It generally does not review admissions decisions ([OIA](https://www.oiahe.org.uk/students/can-you-complain-to-us/)).
- **Scotland and Northern Ireland:** complaints go to the SPSO and NIPSO instead ([UKCISA](https://www.ukcisa.org.uk/student-advice/life-in-the-uk/complaints-about-your-institution/) *(secondary)*).
- **Immigration advisers:** the IAA takes complaints about registered advisers and about advice from unregulated people, normally within 12 months. It cannot get money refunded ([GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/complain-about-an-immigration-adviser)). Check an adviser with the [IAA Adviser Finder](https://portal.oisc.gov.uk/s/adviser-finder).
- **Fraud:** Report Fraud replaced Action Fraud on 4 December 2025, for England, Wales and Northern Ireland ([Report Fraud](https://www.reportfraud.police.uk/)). That date comes from police notices and is **not verified** against an official page.
- **Immigration crime:** report it at [GOV.UK](https://www.gov.uk/report-immigration-crime). Sponsors raise concerns about agents with UKVI's Study Risk Team ([Sponsorship duties 3.29](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/student-sponsor-guidance/sponsorship-duties-accessible)).

## 8. Expert's own obligations in the UK
<!-- keywords: expert uk office, london office, onshore office, our uk branch, register with iaa, iaa registration, supervision, certified counsellors, records, client agreement, uk fair, visiting the uk, expert obligations -->

**Expert's UK office is inside the UK regime. Offshore offices are bound mainly through university contracts and the National Code.** Points marked *(inference)* are Expert-practice readings, not stated rules.

- **UK office:** staff may give individual visa advice only if Expert registers with the IAA at the right level, or they work under a solicitor or registered adviser ([s.84(2)](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/33/section/84)). Otherwise they only signpost (§3) *(inference)*.
- **Directors:** they can be personally liable for unregulated advice given with their consent or through their neglect ([s.91](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1999/33/section/91)).
- **Offshore staff in the UK:** an overseas firm that meets clients in the UK needs regulation ([IAA practice note](https://assets.publishing.service.gov.uk/media/688886abbe2291b14d11b03b/IAA_Immigration_Assistance_practice_note_July_2025.pdf)). Offshore staff at UK events must not give visa advice *(inference)*.
- **Certification:** every counsellor on UK files should hold the British Council certificate ([Code 5.2](https://www.aqf.info/s/The-national-code-for-UK-education-agents-July-2025.pdf)) and renew it before its 2 years run out ([British Council](https://www.britishcouncil.org/education/agents-counsellors/database)). Some partners require at least one certified counsellor in each branch ([Coventry](https://www.coventry.ac.uk/partner-hub/agent-requirements/)).
- **Business practice under the Code:**
  - signed client agreements covering fees, refunds and complaints;
  - Expert's company registration shown on its website and adverts;
  - client records and agreements kept for 2 years;
  - a check for fraudulent documents ([Code 1.5, 2.5, 2.8](https://www.aqf.info/s/The-national-code-for-UK-education-agents-July-2025.pdf)).
- **Refusal record:** universities see Expert on each CAS (§4), so Expert's refusal record follows it to every partner *(inference)*.
