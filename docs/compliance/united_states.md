# United States — Compliance guide for Expert counsellors

*Researched 9 October 2026. Rules marked "as of" can change quickly, so re-check them on the linked official page; anything marked **not verified** could not be confirmed from an official source.*

## 1. Bottom line for counsellors
<!-- keywords: us rules summary, what can i do, us agent rules, bottom line, us compliance, f-1 counselling, allowed, not allowed, red lines, usa -->

**The US does not license education agents, but US law still limits what Expert staff may say and do on F-1 cases.**

- **May:** shortlist schools, help with applications, explain I-20 and visa steps, run honest interview practice ([NACAC Guide](https://www.nacacnet.org/wp-content/uploads/NACAC-Guide-to-Ethical-Practice-in-College-Admission.pdf)).
- **Must not:** give immigration legal advice or represent anyone before DHS for a fee. Only attorneys and accredited representatives may ([USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/scams-fraud-and-misconduct/avoid-scams/common-scams)).
- **Never:** script false answers or pass on a doubtful document. Misrepresentation bars the student with no time limit, and an agent's misstatement counts against a student who knew of it ([9 FAM 302.9](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM030209.html)).
- Tell students which US schools pay Expert (section 5).
- Commission is lawful only for students who live abroad and cannot get US federal aid (section 2).
- Re-check social-media, interview-location and fee rules before each case; they changed in 2025-26 (section 4).

## 2. Federal rules on agents and commission
<!-- keywords: commission legal, incentive compensation, title iv, federal student aid, us citizen abroad, green card holder, bundled services, third-party servicer, study across the pond, per capita payment, higher education act -->

**No federal law licenses education agents. The federal rule that matters is the Title IV ban on enrolment-based pay, which has a foreign-student exception.**

- Federal-aid schools may not pay commission for enrolments ([34 CFR 668.14(b)(22)](https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-34/subtitle-B/chapter-VI/part-668/subpart-B/section-668.14); [20 USC 1094(a)(20)](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/20/1094)).
- The ban does not cover "foreign students residing in foreign countries who are not eligible to receive Federal student assistance" ([LII text](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/668.14)). So a US citizen or green-card holder living in Nepal is not commissionable.
- Feb 2026: a recruiter paid $1.3M under the False Claims Act for commission on aid-funded American students ([DOJ](https://www.justice.gov/opa/pr/international-student-recruiting-firm-and-co-founder-pay-13m-resolve-false-claims-act)); ED's Inspector General issued an audit alert on 21 Jul 2026 ([ED OIG](https://oig.ed.gov/sites/default/files/document/2026-07/fy26_cpa-26-017.21.26v100_508_secured.pdf)).
- The 2023 third-party servicer letter GEN-23-03 was rescinded in Nov 2024 ([FSA](https://fsapartners.ed.gov/knowledge-center/library/dear-colleague-letters/2023-02-15/requirements-and-responsibilities-third-party-servicers-and-institutions-updated-november-14-2024)). Any 2025-26 change to the 2011 bundled-services guidance is **not verified**.
- An agent's misrepresentation counts as the school's own ([34 CFR 668.71](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/668.71)).

## 3. Immigration legal advice and representation
<!-- keywords: immigration lawyer, legal advice, represent, accredited representative, notario, unauthorized practice of law, ds-160 help, fill form, opt advice, change of status, immigration consultant -->

**Before DHS, paid representation is for US attorneys and DOJ-accredited representatives only.**

- Others allowed by [8 CFR 292.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/292.1) are narrow: supervised law students, unpaid "reputable individuals" with a prior link to the person, and foreign attorneys abroad at DHS discretion.
- "Practice" includes advising on the facts and law while preparing papers. Only blank-filling for nominal pay, without claiming legal skill, is excluded ([8 CFR 1.2](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/1.2)).
- USCIS: a notario "is not authorized to provide you with any legal services related to immigration" ([USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/scams-fraud-and-misconduct/avoid-scams/common-scams)). State laws also punish non-lawyer consultants inside the US ([ILRC](https://www.ilrc.org/community-resources/anti-fraud-assistance) *(secondary)*); reach offshore is **not verified**.
- **DS-160 is a grey zone.** A helper must be named on the form, and the applicant signs it under penalty of perjury ([DS-160 FAQ](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/forms/ds-160-online-nonimmigrant-visa-application/ds-160-faqs.html); [22 CFR 41.103](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/22/41.103)). No rule found bars offshore data entry. Enter only the student's own facts *(inference)*.
- Refer OPT, change of status, reinstatement and fraud findings to a US immigration attorney.

## 4. F-1 visa interview and fraud
<!-- keywords: visa interview, f-1 interview, interview prep, coach, coaching, interview answers, mock interview, interview script, 214(b), immigrant intent, misrepresentation, fake documents, social media public, interview location, interview waiver, visa integrity fee, refused visa -->

**Prepare students to tell the truth in their own words; never supply a script, false fact or doubtful document.**

- 214(b): applicants are presumed immigrants until shown otherwise ([8 USC 1184](https://www.law.cornell.edu/uscode/text/8/1184)); no appeal, reapply only with new facts ([DOS](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/us-visas/visa-information-resources/visa-denials.html)).
- 212(a)(6)(C)(i): fraud or wilful material misrepresentation bars the student with no time limit (discretionary waiver only). Using an agent "does not serve to insulate" a student who knew ([9 FAM 302.9](https://fam.state.gov/FAM/09FAM/09FAM030209.html)).
- Officers may ask students to restate things in their own words ([9 FAM 402.5](https://fam.state.gov/fam/09FAM/09FAM040205.html)).
- As of 9 Oct 2026:
  - F/M/J social media must be public, since 18 Jun 2025 ([State](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesperson/2025/06/announcement-of-expanded-screening-and-vetting-for-visa-applicants)).
  - Interview in the country of nationality or residence, since 6 Sep 2025 ([DOS](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/adjudicating-niv-applicants-in-their-country-of-residence.html)).
  - F-1 interviews are generally in person from 1 Oct 2025 ([DOS](https://travel.state.gov/content/travel/en/News/visas-news/interview-waiver-update-sept-18-2025.html)).
  - The $250 visa integrity fee is law ([Fed. Reg.](https://www.federalregister.gov/d/2025-13738)). Only some posts collected it as of May 2026 ([BU](https://www.bu.edu/isso/2026/05/19/visa-integrity-fee/) *(secondary)*); collection everywhere is **not verified**.

## 5. Professional standards: AIRC and NACAC
<!-- keywords: airc, certification, certified agency, nacac, ethical practice, commissioned agents, agent contract, disclose commission, double dipping, standards, us university agent agreement -->

**US universities that use agents rely on AIRC certification, NACAC ethics and their own agent contracts.**

- AIRC has 40 sub-standards in 5 areas: organisation, recruitment integrity, students and families, institutions, complaints. They require visible fees and refunds, a written student agreement, no fees contingent on scholarships, no guarantees without facts, student-written essays and no false records ([AIRC](https://airc-education.org/agency-standards)).
- To apply, an agency needs 2 years of trading, at least 25 students placed and no fraud finding in the last 7 years ([AIRC FAQ](https://www.airc-education.org/faqs)). The 5-year term is **not verified**.
- NACAC has allowed commissioned agents for recruiting outside the US since 2013 ([NACAC](https://nacacnet.org/wp-content/uploads/2022/10/nacac-agents-part-1-trends-0421_2.pdf)). Its 2026 Guide limits per-student pay to students abroad who cannot get federal aid, and requires schools to hold agents to staff ethics and disclose that they use agents ([Guide](https://www.nacacnet.org/wp-content/uploads/NACAC-Guide-to-Ethical-Practice-in-College-Admission.pdf)).
- Since 2016, schools must make sure agents tell students which schools pay them ([PIE](https://thepienews.com/nacac-amends-code-of-ethics-to-improve-transparency-in-agent-use/) *(secondary)*).
- Each university's agent contract is binding. Follow it.

## 6. Complaints and escalation
<!-- keywords: complaint, report fraud, report scam, fake agent, visa fraud tip, ftc, attorney general, report to university, airc complaint, escalate -->

**Send each problem to the body that can act on it, and keep a written record.**

- **Visa fraud:** the State Department's online tip portal (launched Sep 2026, anonymous allowed) ([State](https://www.state.gov/releases/office-of-the-spokesman/2026/09/launch-of-online-visa-fraud-tip-portal/); [form](https://travel.state.gov/en/report-visa-fraud.html)). Fraud in a specific case can also go to the embassy that handles it ([DSS](https://dsscrimetips.state.gov/visa-fraud-tip/)).
- **Scams and notarios:** the FTC or a state consumer office ([USCIS](https://www.uscis.gov/scams-fraud-and-misconduct/avoid-scams/common-scams); [FTC](https://reportfraud.ftc.gov/)). State attorneys general enforce consumer law ([NAAG](https://www.naag.org/find-my-ag/)).
- **AIRC-certified agency:** a signed written comment naming the agency and the standard breached, for conduct in the last 7 years; AIRC sends it to the agency for a response ([AIRC](https://www.airc-education.org/s/AIRC-Agency_Comment-Guidelines.pdf)).
- **A school's agent:** the school, which NACAC says must "respond quickly and decisively" ([NACAC Guide](https://www.nacacnet.org/wp-content/uploads/NACAC-Guide-to-Ethical-Practice-in-College-Admission.pdf)).
- **Fake lawyers or representatives:** the state bar, after checking the [EOIR roster](https://www.justice.gov/eoir/recognition-accreditation-roster-reports).
- **Inside Expert:** the branch manager, with a CRM note *(inference)*.

## 7. Expert's own obligations for US cases
<!-- keywords: expert policy, our obligations, disclosure to student, commission disclosure, written agreement, refer to attorney, us partner contract, record keeping, staff training, airc certified -->

**Expert's US duties come from university contracts and AIRC/NACAC norms, not a US licence. Items marked *(inference)* are Edge's reading, not a quoted rule.**

- Tell each US-bound student which universities pay Expert ([PIE on NACAC](https://thepienews.com/nacac-amends-code-of-ethics-to-improve-transparency-in-agent-use/) *(secondary)*). Charging the student and taking commission for the same service is a conflict *(inference)*.
- Give every student a written agreement listing services, fees and refunds ([AIRC Std 3](https://airc-education.org/agency-standards)).
- Check citizenship and residence before treating a lead as commissionable. US citizens, green-card holders and US residents fall outside the exception ([34 CFR 668.14](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/34/668.14)).
- Refer DHS matters (OPT, change of status, reinstatement) to a US immigration attorney ([8 CFR 292.1](https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/8/292.1)).
- Keep student records for at least 3 years. Essays must be the student's own work ([AIRC](https://airc-education.org/agency-standards)).
- Whether Expert holds AIRC certification is **not verified**. Do not claim it without checking AIRC's directory.
- Never claim a special link to a US embassy *(inference)*.
