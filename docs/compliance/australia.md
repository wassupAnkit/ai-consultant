# Australia — Compliance guide for Expert counsellors

*Researched 9 October 2026. Rules are stated as at that date, and time-sensitive points carry "as of"; anything marked "not verified" could not be confirmed against an official source and must not be told to students as fact.*

## 1. Bottom line for counsellors
<!-- keywords: can i, is this allowed, red lines, never coach, australia compliance, quick summary, what should i do, guarantee, promise visa, mislead, australian law -->

**Recruit and counsel honestly, in the student's best interest. Never chase money the law forbids, and never give visa advice in Australia unless you are a registered migration agent (RMA).**

- Never seek, negotiate or push back for commission, cash or any other benefit on an onshore transfer. Since 1 April 2026 the receiving provider may not pay it ([National Code 4.7](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00033/asmade/text)). A provider that refuses is obeying the law (§4).
- Never steer a student to change provider for an incentive. The receiving provider must be satisfied the transfer is in the student's best interests ([TEQSA/ASQA alert, 13 Jul 2026](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/sector-updates-and-alerts/joint-asqa-and-teqsa-sector-alert-compliance-concerns-about-overseas-student-transfers)).
- Never arrange a transfer inside the restriction period without a release. The period is 12 months as of 9 Oct 2026 (§3, [F2026L01351](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01351/asmade/text)).
- Never give immigration assistance in Australia unless you are an RMA or a lawyer. Doing so is an offence, and charging for it carries up to 10 years' jail ([Migration Act s280–281](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/latest/text)). Refer to an Expert RMA (§7).
- Never promise a visa, admission or migration outcome, and never give false or misleading information ([National Code Std 1, 4](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text)).
- **Do instead:** check the student's months completed and release status, explain the options honestly, and refer visa questions to an RMA. If the student still wants help with a transfer, Expert may charge the student a fee ([Study Australia](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/Agent-Hub/agent-news-index/new-rules-on-agent-commissions-for-onshore-student-transfers)).

## 2. ESOS Act and National Code Standard 4: education agent obligations
<!-- keywords: standard 4, esos act, national code, agent obligations, agent agreement, written agreement, our agreement, provider responsibility, prisms, agent list, corrective action, terminate agent, conflict of interest, false or misleading, standard 1, education agent definition -->

**Providers are legally responsible for their agents, so an agent's duties arrive through its contract with each provider.**

- The provider must sign a written agreement with each agent and record the agent in PRISMS. The agreement must cover monitoring, corrective action and the grounds for termination ([Std 4.1–4.2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text); [Dept Std 4 guide](https://www.education.gov.au/download/11456/standard-4-education-agents/43665/document/pdf)).
- The provider must require its agents to:
  - declare and avoid conflicts of interest;
  - keep confidentiality and transparency;
  - act honestly, in good faith and in the student's best interests;
  - know the Agent Code of Ethics ([Std 4.3](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text)).
- If an agent breaches these duties, the provider must act at once. For false or misleading recruitment, it must end the agreement, or make the agent drop the staff member responsible ([Std 4.4–4.5](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text)).
- A provider must not accept students from an agent it suspects of unauthorised migration advice, dishonest recruitment (including against Standard 7) or non-genuine enrolments ([Std 4.6](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text)).
- Providers must publish a list of all their agents on their website ([ESOS s21A](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00757/latest/text)).
- An "education agent" is any entity, in or outside Australia, that recruits, advises on enrolment or otherwise deals with overseas students for a provider and is not the provider's permanent employee ([ESOS s6BA, from 5 Dec 2025](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2025A00074/asmade/text)).
- Standard 1 bars claiming to secure a migration outcome or guaranteeing an education-assessment outcome ([Std 1](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text)).

## 3. Onshore student transfers: Standard 7
<!-- keywords: standard 7, transfer restriction, 12 months, six months, release letter, letter of release, change provider, switch college, change university, transfer request, refused release, compassionate, principal course, lower level course, new student visa -->

**As of 9 Oct 2026, a receiving provider must not knowingly enrol a transferring student until the student has completed 12 months of the principal course with the releasing provider, unless an exception applies.**

- 12 months applies 2 Oct 2026 to 30 June 2027, then 6 months again from 1 July 2027; students leaving a school stay at 6 months ([Std 7.1, F2026L01351](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01351/asmade/text)).
- A student who had done 6–12 months on 2 Oct 2026 is restricted again until reaching 12 months ([explanatory statement](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01351/asmade/downloads)).
- **Exceptions:**
  - the releasing provider or course is no longer registered;
  - a sanction stops the student continuing;
  - the releasing provider agrees to the release and records it in PRISMS;
  - a government sponsor supports the move in writing ([Std 7.1A](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01351/asmade/text)).
- Best-interest release grounds include poor progress despite intervention, compassionate or compelling circumstances, course not delivered as agreed, unmet reasonable expectations, or a student misled by an agent ([Std 7.2](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text)).
- A release must be free. A refusal must be in writing, with reasons and a right to appeal within 20 working days ([Std 7](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text)).
- Moving to a lower-level course or to ELICOS needs a new Student visa ([Study Australia](https://www.studyaustralia.gov.au/en/plan-your-studies/changing-your-course-or-provider)); that page still says 6 months (9 Oct 2026), the instrument governs.
- Since 2 Oct 2026, current Student visa holders generally must lodge a further Student visa from outside Australia; exempt classes **not verified** ([F2026L01347](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01347/asmade/downloads)). Refer to an RMA.
- A new visa before any transfer is planned for mid-2027: **not yet law** ([statement](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L01351/asmade/downloads)).

## 4. The 2026 ban on commission for onshore student transfers
<!-- keywords: commission, onshore transfer, transfer commission, refuse commission, university won't pay, college won't pay, push back, no commission, bonus, non-monetary benefit, marketing fee, fee for service, packaged course, further study, grandfathering, 31 march 2026, 1 april 2026 -->

**A provider must not give an education agent commission for recruiting an overseas student who has started a course with another registered provider ([National Code 4.7](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00033/asmade/text)).**

- **Instrument:** National Code Amendment (Education Agent Commissions) Instrument 2026 (F2026L00033), made under ESOS s33, in force 21 Jan 2026, backing the Education Legislation Amendment (Integrity and Other Measures) Act 2025.
- **Start date:** commission stays allowed if the new provider accepted the student on or before 31 March 2026 (Std 4.8.1). The ban therefore applies to acceptances from 1 April 2026 ([TEQSA, 4 Jun 2026](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/about-us/news-and-events/latest-news/education-agents-and-commissions)). "31 March" reports describe the same cut-off.
- **Scope:** from the student's start with the first provider, including after withdrawal or cancellation, until the principal course is completed ([explanatory statement](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2026L00033/asmade/downloads)).
- **Not a transfer:**
  - the next course in the package on the visa CoEs;
  - a course starting after the principal course is completed, e.g. bachelor's then master's (Std 4.8.2–4.8.3).
  - A similar course at a different provider is still a transfer.
- "Commission" is any monetary or non-monetary benefit to the agent or an associate: fees, bonuses, gifts, discounts, incentives ([ESOS s6BB](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2025A00074/asmade/text)).
- **Liability:** the duty is the provider's. Regulators call any attempt by providers or agents to bypass the ban unacceptable ([TEQSA/ASQA alert](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/sector-updates-and-alerts/joint-asqa-and-teqsa-sector-alert-compliance-concerns-about-overseas-student-transfers)).
- **So:** a provider refusing commission is complying with the law. Do not push back, and do not ask for a "marketing fee" or any other benefit. Expert may still help the student and charge the student directly.

## 5. Commission reporting and provider visibility of agent outcomes
<!-- keywords: commission reporting, commission data, prisms data, agent performance, visa refusal rate, completion rate, agent data, data sharing, section 21b, reporting period, agent track record, ownership disclosure -->

**Expert's commissions and student outcomes are reported to the government and increasingly visible to every provider.**

- The Department can require any provider to report, for each agent, the total dollars paid, the value and description of non-monetary benefits, and the number of students recruited. Not complying is a strict-liability offence (60 penalty units). The power covers commissions given from 5 Dec 2025 ([ESOS s21B](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2025A00074/asmade/text)).
- The first collection covers 1 Jan–30 Jun 2026 ([Dept fact sheet](https://www.education.gov.au/download/20661/agent-commissions-data-collection/45800/principal-executive-officer-fact-sheet/pdf)). Due date **not verified**.
- The government may give providers agent-level data:
  - visa applications granted, refused, withdrawn or invalid;
  - visa cancellations, enrolments and completion rates;
  - since Dec 2025, transfer counts and commission information ([ESOS s175](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00757/latest/text)).
- ASQA says providers will see this data through PRISMS, and that agents and the public will not ([ASQA FAQ, 4 Dec 2025](https://www.asqa.gov.au/sites/default/files/2025-12/ESOS%20Act%20amendments%20FAQs%20-%20Version%201.pdf)). It is reported to cover all agents used by all providers ([ICEF Monitor](https://monitor.icef.com/2026/02/australia-moving-to-wider-sharing-of-education-agent-data/) *(secondary)*). Go-live date **not verified**.
- A provider must tell its regulator within 10 business days if it owns or controls an agent, or an agent owns or controls it ([ESOS s17A(4A)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2025A00074/asmade/text)).
- **In practice:** refusals, transfers and weak files follow Expert to every partner provider.

## 6. Agent quality frameworks: ACE, QEAC and registration
<!-- keywords: agent code of ethics, ace, qeac, eatc, aeatc, agent training, certification, neas, endorsed agent, agent registration, quality framework, accreditation, licence, professional development -->

**As of 9 Oct 2026 we found no government registration or licensing scheme for education agents in Australia. That absence is not verified. Quality is set by the Agent Code of Ethics, by training and by provider contracts.**

- The Agent Code of Ethics (ACE) has 7 principles ([ACE](https://internationaleducation.gov.au/news/latest-news/Documents/Australian%20International%20Education%20and%20Training%20-%20Agent%20Code%20of%20Ethics.pdf)). They include:
  - acting in the student's best interests;
  - being open about student fees and provider commissions;
  - giving accurate, tailored information;
  - written agreements with both the student and the provider;
  - protecting minors.
- Providers must require their agents to understand the ACE ([Std 4.3](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text)). Its staff-capability standard expects agent training.
- QEAC is awarded on passing the ICEF Academy Education Agent Training Course (EATC, originally PIER) exam ([ICEF](https://www.icef.com/academy/courses/education-agent-training-course-eatc/) *(industry)*).
- NEAS lists Expert as a NEAS Endorsed Education Agent, assessed against Quality Area J ([NEAS](https://neas.org.au/?p=26195) *(industry body)*).
- **Government powers:**
  - ESOS s21A(2) lets regulations set requirements for providers about their agents ([ESOS](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2004A00757/latest/text)).
  - The 2025 Act widened definitions and reporting but created no agent register ([Act](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2025A00074/asmade/text)).
- An Australian "Agent Quality Framework" like the UK's is **not verified**. The ISEAA industry body proposed a "Registered Student Visa Advisor" certification ([ISEAA](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/reports-and-pubs/files/world-class-submissions/submission-international-student-education-agents-association.pdf) *(industry submission)*).

## 7. Migration advice boundary: immigration assistance and RMAs
<!-- keywords: migration advice, immigration assistance, visa advice, which visa, visa options, after graduation, onshore visa, registered migration agent, rma, marn, omara, mara, 485, graduate visa, pr pathway, visa refusal, visa cancellation, appeal, lawyer, exempt person, offshore advice -->

**In Australia only RMAs, Australian legal practitioners and exempt persons may give immigration assistance ([Migration Act s280](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/latest/text)).**

- **Immigration assistance** means using migration knowledge to help a visa or cancellation-review applicant: preparing the application, advising on it, or preparing for or appearing in court or ART proceedings. It also covers sponsorship and nomination ([s276](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/latest/text)).
- **Not assistance:** clerical work, translation, telling someone they must apply for a visa, or passing on another party's information without substantial comment ([s276(3)](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C1958A00062/latest/text)).
- **Penalties:** 60 penalty units. Asking for or taking a fee for it, including for a non-RMA's help, carries up to 10 years' jail (s281). Education agents are not exempt ([OMARA](https://www.mara.gov.au/get-help-visa-subsite/FIles/giving_immigration_assistance_in_australia_english.pdf)).
- **Lawyers:** since 22 Mar 2021, lawyers with a practising certificate need not register ([Home Affairs](https://www.homeaffairs.gov.au/reports-and-pubs/files/migrations-agents-instruments-review-report/T3_2_Fact_sheet_Offshore_immigration_assistance.pdf)). The current Act has no separate "immigration legal assistance" section.
- **Offshore:** Part 3 has no extraterritorial effect, so the offence does not reach advice given outside Australia (same source). Expert should still refer; this is Expert practice, not law.
- **Refer to an RMA:** onshore visa options, the 485, PR pathways, refusals, ART appeals, cancellation notices, visa conditions and expiry.
- **Check a MARN:** mara.gov.au, "Search for a registered agent" ([OMARA guide](https://www.mara.gov.au/get-help-visa-subsite/Files/find-registered-migration-agent.pdf)).
- **RMA Code of Conduct:** the Migration (Migration Agents Code of Conduct) Regulations 2021 ([F2021L01856](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L01856/latest/text); §9).

## 8. Complaints and escalation
<!-- keywords: complaint, complain, ombudsman, overseas students ombudsman, internal appeal, appeal transfer refusal, tps, tuition protection, provider closed, college closed, closed, closure, refund, report agent, border watch, omara complaint, tip-off, teqsa, asqa -->

**Start with the provider's own process, then go to the external body.**

- A student's complaint against a provider goes to the provider's complaints and appeals process first. After that:
  - for private providers, the Overseas Students Ombudsman;
  - for public providers, the state or territory ombudsman. The Commonwealth Ombudsman is free ([Ombudsman fact sheet](https://www.ombudsman.gov.au/__data/assets/pdf_file/0033/79683/Factsheet_student_education-agents-links-fixed-A1576258.pdf)).
- Complaints about an education agent go to the provider that engages it, then to the Ombudsman (same source).
- For a refused transfer, the student has 20 working days to appeal. The provider cannot finalise the refusal in PRISMS until the appeal is done ([Std 7](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text)).
- If a provider closes or stops a course, it must place or refund the student within 14 days. The Tuition Protection Service (TPS) then helps find another course or refunds unspent tuition ([TPS](https://www.education.gov.au/tps/international-students)).
- **RMA complaints:** try the agent first, then OMARA's complaint form. A complaint does not affect the visa ([OMARA consumer guide](https://www.mara.gov.au/get-help-visa-subsite/FIles/consumer_guide_english.pdf)).
- **Unlawful immigration assistance:** report it through Border Watch Online ([OMARA](https://www.mara.gov.au/get-help-visa-subsite/FIles/giving_immigration_assistance_in_australia_english.pdf)).
- **Providers getting round the commission ban:** use ASQA's VET Tip-Off Line or TEQSA's complaints process ([TEQSA/ASQA alert](https://www.teqsa.gov.au/guides-resources/resources/sector-updates-and-alerts/joint-asqa-and-teqsa-sector-alert-compliance-concerns-about-overseas-student-transfers)).
- The ACE expects Expert to run a clear, fair complaints and appeals process ([ACE](https://internationaleducation.gov.au/news/latest-news/Documents/Australian%20International%20Education%20and%20Training%20-%20Agent%20Code%20of%20Ethics.pdf)).

## 9. Expert's own obligations in Australia
<!-- keywords: our obligations, agency obligations, expert obligations, our agency, partner agreement, provider contract, employing rma, employer of rma, supervise staff, marn display, conflict disclosure, service agreement, record keeping, internal policy -->

**As an education agent, the law binds Expert's partner providers. Expert's own duties arrive through each partner agreement, and a breach can cost the partnership.**

- **Contract duties:** act honestly and in the student's best interests, and declare conflicts of interest. On a breach the provider must act; on false recruitment it must terminate ([Std 4.3–4.5](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2017L01182/latest/text)).
- Offshore branches are "education agents" too ([ESOS s6BA](https://www.legislation.gov.au/C2025A00074/asmade/text)). Expert's commissions and outcomes are reportable (§5).

**As an employer of RMAs, the Code of Conduct binds each RMA ([F2021L01856](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2021L01856/latest/text)):**

- s23: an RMA takes reasonable steps so that no one in the business gives unlawful immigration assistance.
- s24: an RMA checks the quality of non-RMA staff work it relies on.
- s25: at least one RMA's MARN appears on the business website, social media and immigration ads.
- s26: no false claims, and no claim to be able to procure a visa decision, including by staff.
- s34: written notice of any conflicting interest, and the client's written consent.
- s42: a signed service agreement before giving assistance.
- From 1 Apr 2026, RMAs need annual ethics and Code of Conduct training ([Minister, 17 Mar 2026](https://minister.homeaffairs.gov.au/JulianHill/Pages/new-legislation-strengthens-regulation-registered-migration-agents.aspx)).

**Expert practice, not law (inference):**

- Record each transfer's reason against the Std 7.2 grounds.
- Never let commission decide advice.
- Treat provider commission as an interest an RMA discloses under s34.
