# Australia: Facts Register (student, guardian and graduate visas)

*Dated, cited facts for Edge answers. Checked 9 October 2026. Each value is quoted as published. Primary sources are Department of Home Affairs pages and the Home Affairs price-list service behind the "Current visa pricing table", the Federal Register of Legislation, and the Administrative Review Tribunal (ART). A row marked "(secondary — verify)" could not be confirmed on a government page and must be checked before a counsellor relies on it. OSHC premiums are indicative market prices, not government figures.*

Notes
- **Siblings, uncles and other relatives cannot be the income source on the annual income route.** The instrument names only the "parent, spouse or de facto partner". If both parents work, their incomes can be combined. Evidence must be official government documents such as tax assessments less than 12 months old. Bank statements and letters from employers are not accepted. Other relatives can still fund a student through the deposits route, with proof of relationship, identity and source of funds. Sources: [LIN 19/198](https://www.legislation.gov.au/F2019L01366/latest/text); [Home Affairs: Student visa 500](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500).
- **Genuine access matters as much as the amount.** A bank balance certificate for a single day does not show how the funds were built up. Explain where deposits came from, and show disbursement for loans. Source: [Home Affairs: Student visa 500](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500).
- Financial evidence may be requested even if the Document Checklist Tool does not require it. Source: [Home Affairs: Student visa 500](https://immi.homeaffairs.gov.au/visas/getting-a-visa/visa-listing/student-500).
- The travel amounts are a published guide, not a figure set in the legislative instrument. LIN 19/198 only says "travel expenses".
